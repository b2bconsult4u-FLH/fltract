Florida Flood Risk: King Tides, Stormwater and Property Decisions

Questions to ask before buying, selling or improving Florida property

Educational information only. Verify current records and requirements with the responsible agency.

Flooding is not limited to hurricanes

Coastal flooding can occur during unusually high tides, particularly when persistent onshore winds and rainfall coincide. Inland parcels can also face flooding from intense rain, drainage limitations and nearby waterways.

Start with the official flood map

Use FEMA's Flood Map Service Center to locate the parcel and review the current effective map. Flood-zone designations help identify mapped risk and insurance requirements, but a property outside a mapped high-risk zone can still flood.

Check local evidence

Ask the city or county floodplain administrator about historic flooding, road access, drainage complaints, permits and local elevation requirements. Visit during or after heavy rainfall when practical, and request available elevation information.

Understand the insurance question

Obtain property-specific flood-insurance quotes and ask your lender what coverage may be required. Premiums and eligibility depend on individual circumstances and can change.

Consider future changes

Coastal tides, sea-level trends, development and drainage infrastructure can affect future exposure. A single map or past dry season is not a guarantee against flooding.

Before a transaction

Document disclosures, investigate any water intrusion or previous flood claims where available, and seek advice from qualified floodplain, insurance, surveying and engineering professionals as needed.

Official starting points

  • FEMA Flood Map Service Center
  • NOAA Tides and Currents
  • Florida Flood Hub
