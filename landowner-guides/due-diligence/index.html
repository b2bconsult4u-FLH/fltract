Florida Property Due Diligence: What to Verify Before You Commit

A practical checklist for landowners and buyers

Educational information only. Verify current records and requirements with the responsible agency.

Confirm the parcel and ownership

Start with the county property appraiser and clerk of court. Match the parcel identifier, legal description and recorded ownership to the property being discussed. Property appraiser records are useful starting points, but they are not a substitute for a title examination or survey.

Verify access and physical boundaries

A road visible on a map is not proof of legal access. Ask for recorded easements, road-maintenance agreements and a current survey. Check whether wetlands, drainage features, utilities or neighboring uses affect the buildable area.

Research zoning and permitted use

Contact the local planning or zoning department about the parcel's current designation, comprehensive-plan future land use, setbacks, permitted uses, special exceptions and any pending changes. Do not assume that an advertised commercial or agricultural use is automatically permitted.

Understand water and environmental constraints

Review FEMA's flood mapping, local drainage records, wetlands information and any relevant environmental permits. A mapped boundary is a screening tool, not a site-specific engineering determination.

Estimate total transaction and ownership costs

Consider taxes, special assessments, impact fees, utilities, road improvements, surveys, environmental reviews and financing costs. Ask qualified professionals for project-specific estimates.

Document what remains unknown

Keep a written question list and request original records before committing funds. For transactions, consult appropriate licensed real estate, legal, surveying and engineering professionals.

Official starting points

  • Florida Department of Revenue — Property Tax
  • FEMA Flood Map Service Center
  • Florida Department of Environmental Protection
